# Psychology content and resource review

Reviewed: 12 October 2026. Release scope: an English-first adult app for Brazil and the United States.

This is a research and product-content audit by development agents. It is not a professional psychological opinion, treatment, or certification. It does not represent the American Psychological Association (APA), the Brazilian Conselho Federal de Psicologia (CFP), or any listed publisher. The relevant Brazilian body is the CFP and its Regional Councils, rather than a “National Psychology Council.”

## Default guide changes

The 17-section English and Portuguese built-in templates were rewritten as secular personal organization and everyday care. Version 2 preserves all existing section, field, ladder, ladder-row, schedule, and schedule-row IDs; the before/after comparison passed. Existing responses and custom guides must remain intact when persistence migrates a built-in template. The old templates were saved in a temporary comparison snapshot outside the repository.

The English guide explicitly addresses adults aged 18 and older and describes US primary care and licensed professional options alongside Brazilian UBS/CAPS routes. This text does not promise eligibility, appointment availability, or free treatment. Culture, disability, material constraints, diverse relationships, and user-defined goals are part of the framing.

Theology and theology review are off by default; default prayer, psalm, and scripture content is empty. Future spiritual reflection requires explicit personal choice, including the choice of no belief. Psychological care is not subordinated to religious doctrine.

A map presents cognitive/behavioral, psychodynamic/psychoanalytic, humanistic/phenomenological/existential, systemic/social/community, and Schema Therapy perspectives without selecting a treatment for a person. Plural presentation does not mean equal evidence for every condition, intervention, or outcome. The app should not claim that an approach or quotation is universally accepted.

Removed content includes a preassigned clinical profile, moral verdicts, compulsory frequency, historical fixed appointments, and marriage or family as everyone's assumed goal. A self-directed trauma-exposure ladder involving a specific building was replaced with planning support, work needs, and boundaries. Everyday participation examples are optional and adaptable rather than exposure protocols. Attention exercises may be skipped or stopped; the guide does not process traumatic memories.

Diary ratings are subjective reflections. They have no diagnostic thresholds, screening interpretations, clinical scores, or claims of improvement. Activity counts describe records, not health, worth, or performance. The English narrative prompt asks what happened in the person's own words rather than requiring an “objective” account.

## English-only library

The catalogue contains 25 distinct English destinations: 12 US publishers, 6 UK publishers, 3 Australian publishers, and 4 international WHO resources. Region identifies the publisher's jurisdiction, not launch-market coverage or eligibility for care. English information may be read in Brazil or the United States; UK/Australian service instructions and local legal procedures are not transferred to those markets.

Titles, descriptions, access notes, tags, and declared content language are English. Portuguese-only SUS/CFP/CVV links and Spanish PAHO catalogue pages were removed from the app library. General SciELO, PePSIC, and BVS portals were also removed because an English interface or abstract does not establish a complete English destination.

Each entry describes a specific use and access condition. Free reading is distinguished from treatment cost, service eligibility, course registration, and group availability. CCI materials identify their cognitive/behavioral or compassion orientation and remain optional. No approach is ranked, endorsed, or chosen as a default treatment.

PubMed opens a search restricted to English publications and free full-text links, using `english[lang]` and `free full text[sb]`. The [official PubMed documentation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/help/) distinguishes a publication's language from a translated English abstract and explains the full-text subset. A user still needs to follow the free publisher/PMC link; changing the query can remove these restrictions. Studies are research reading, not individual clinical advice.

Only brief original descriptions and links are stored. Publications, worksheets, scales, images, and institutional logos were not copied or adapted. No crisis or suicide/self-harm helpline number was added to this catalogue.

## Brazilian and international audit references

These Portuguese Brazilian pages are internal audit references and are not app-library destinations:

- [CFP: Resolution 7/2023 and secular professional practice](https://site.cfp.org.br/cfp-publica-resolucao-sobre-laicidade-no-exercicio-da-psicologia/) informed respect for religious, agnostic, and atheist experience and the prohibition on religious induction in professional practice. The secular app default is a product-design inference from that guidance and the user's preference, not a claim of CFP approval.
- [Ministry of Health: Psychosocial Care Network](https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/composicao/saes/desmad/raps) informed the Brazilian context of autonomy, human rights, equity, primary care, and community support.
- [Ministry of Health: CAPS](https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/composicao/saes/desmad/raps/caps/caps) informed the concise description of community mental health care; local units and hours still require confirmation.
- [CFP professional register](https://cadastro.cfp.org.br/) is an internal reference for the distinction between registration and endorsing a particular therapy.

The [APA overview of psychotherapy approaches](https://www.apa.org/topics/psychotherapy/approaches), [WHO human-rights overview](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/mental-health-promoting-and-protecting-human-rights), and [APA racism/bias information](https://www.apa.org/topics/racism-bias-discrimination) inform the plural, autonomous, culturally aware framing. They do not establish a treatment plan for an individual or remove local professional and legal requirements.

## Optional onboarding reflections

`lib/onboarding-quotes.ts` contains two short attributed original English excerpts:

- Kristin Neff: 16 words, checked directly in the introductory heading on [her original website](https://self-compassion.org/). The page is undated; the review date must not be shown as its publication date. This site also offers paid services and a self-compassion test; citation is not a recommendation to purchase a service or take a test.
- William James: 9 words, checked in the original English text of [The Principles of Psychology, Volume I](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/57628/pg57628-images.html#Page_402), Chapter XI, “Attention,” page 402. The work was published in 1890; the accessible edition is a 1918 reprint.

Each source contributes fewer than 25 quoted words. These are optional reflections, not universal clinical consensus, promises, therapy preferences, or endorsements of the app. James's observation about attention must not imply that people choose their distress. The popular Rogers self-acceptance quotation was omitted: its wording varies in circulation, and the original page could not be opened for direct confirmation.

Onboarding questions concern visit intention, pace, activity preference, and support preference. They must remain optional, unscored, and outside a health assessment. Answers should stay in memory until the person gives the applicable storage consent; onboarding does not infer diagnosis, trauma history, or religion.

## Implementation boundaries

1. AI output must not diagnose, recommend treatment, evaluate fitness, promise recovery, or treat usage statistics as clinical evidence.
2. Templates must not infer a condition, trauma, belief, relationship status, or therapeutic goal for the person.
3. Spiritual content must require separate opt-in for generation, review, and display. Declining it is a complete choice.
4. Reports of violence require care for safety and support, without instructions to confront an unsafe person, reconcile, forgive, or dispute the person's account.
5. Template migrations must preserve responses, schedules, snapshots, and custom guides.
6. Opening a resource must not transmit journal entries, onboarding answers, or other personal content. External destinations have their own privacy terms.
7. Mobile packaging does not establish clinical efficacy, encryption, crisis monitoring, or regulatory clearance. Legal and native-data-storage review are separate workstreams.
